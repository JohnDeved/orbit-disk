Orbit Disk 0.9.8 discovers files into memory while saving the map to disk in the background. You can browse, search, collect and remove eligible files as soon as they appear, without waiting for their inventory records to finish saving.

## Mapping improvements

- Fresh native scans discover metadata independently of database writes. Views combine pending records with committed rows without omissions or double counting.
- Switching locations keeps the mapping session and its pending work. Existing saved subtrees remain available for reuse, including maps published while discovery is running.
- Removing visible files keeps unrelated pending records available. Removal intents remain durable before filesystem changes, and hard-link allocation stays consistent as records move from memory to the database.
- Small offscreen folders finish their initial listing in one transaction. Larger folders still expose their first batch immediately.
- Discovery reuses its native enumeration buffer, the writer reuses secure directory handles, and cache filenames avoid repeated string formatting. Ancestor identity and symlink checks remain enabled.

## Measured speedup

In the final paired tests over a warm Documents tree containing 178,755 files:

| Actual app model | Before final optimization | 0.9.8 implementation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Complete inventory available, median | 2.066 s | 1.681 s |
| Discovery finished, range | 1.78–1.79 s | 1.45–1.46 s |
| First usable rows, range | 49–51 ms | 45–51 ms |
| Saving and publication finished, range | 10.16–14.60 s | 9.54–9.94 s |

The complete inventory became available about 19% sooner. This comparison isolates the final optimization against the earlier **unreleased concurrent pipeline**, not against 0.9.7. Each variant ran twice in alternating order with a fresh process and empty cache, on Apple M5 with macOS 27.0.1 and Swift 6.3.3. Counts and logical/allocated sizes matched across all eight core/model runs. The measurements include app-model previews but exclude SwiftUI rendering. Saving times vary; these warm samples do not predict cold or whole-Mac performance. [Methods, earlier experiments and results](https://github.com/JohnDeved/orbit-disk/blob/v0.9.8/TECHNICAL-NOTES.md#concurrent-discovery-and-persistence-098).

## Progress and recovery

Saving runs throughout discovery. The pending queue is bounded to 128 MiB of estimated payload; discovery waits if it fills. This is not a total app-memory limit. SQLite FULL synchronization and deletion safeguards remain enabled. A crash preserves committed progress, while unsaved observations and unfinished listings can be rediscovered. Resumed maps, incremental refresh and unsupported native filesystems keep their existing mapping paths.

## Validation

The production build, 32 core checks, asynchronous mapping suite and 18 existing regression binaries passed locally. Coverage includes interrupted scans, cache reuse, buffer reuse, renamed ancestors, hard links, deletion during mapping and RAM/database snapshot races. All 20 experiment inventory databases passed integrity and parentage checks. The release workflow also runs the full cross-platform check suite before publishing the universal app and signed updater feed.
